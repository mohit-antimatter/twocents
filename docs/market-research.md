# Market research: expense tracking apps — what users love, where they break

*Compiled 2026-08-12 from three parallel research sweeps: (1) loved features & retention drivers, (2) complaints & churn, (3) the couples/shared segment & market gaps. Sources are review roundups, app-store review themes, Reddit sentiment (via aggregators — Reddit blocks direct crawling), Trustpilot, and retention research syntheses. Quantitative stats are directional, not audited.*

---

## 1. What people love (ranked by frequency of praise)

| # | Loved quality | Job-to-be-done | Best-in-class |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Zero-effort tracking (sync that just works) | "Show me my money without a second job" | Monarch, PocketGuard |
| 2 | One glanceable "can I spend this?" number | Decision support at the moment of spending | PocketGuard "In My Pocket", YNAB category balance |
| 3 | An opinionated method that changes behavior | "Make me intentional" — a forward plan, not a mirror | YNAB (cult status is the method, not the software) |
| 4 | Emotional payoff: less money anxiety, guilt-free spending | Convert anxiety → calm; this is the real retention hook | YNAB (claims 92% feel less stress) |
| 5 | Couples sharing at no extra cost | "Get us on the same page, stop the money fights" | Monarch (free 2nd seat = its #1 cited differentiator) |
| 6 | Accurate, learning auto-categorization | "Categories match my mental model, no cleanup" | Copilot |
| 7 | Beautiful, fast, native-feeling design | A daily-check app must be pleasant to open | Copilot (4.8★), Ivy Wallet/Cashew among manual apps |
| 8 | Whole financial picture (net worth + budget) | One place; progress on the big number | Monarch, Empower |
| 9 | Recurring bill/subscription detection + calendar | "Never be surprised by a charge"; instant quantifiable wins | Monarch, Copilot, Cashew |
| 10 | Visible goal progress / sinking funds | Turn discipline into a story with a payoff | YNAB targets |
| 11 | Fast manual entry + privacy + no subscription (passionate minority) | Intentionality + trust: no bank creds, data is mine | Cashew, Ivy Wallet, Money Manager (10M+ downloads) |
| 12 | Deep customization + searchable history | "My budget reflects my life"; financial memory | Monarch; Mint's custom categories were its most-mourned feature |

### Retention findings (years-long users vs 3-week abandoners)

- **Category baseline is brutal:** ~38% D30 retention; top-10 finance apps lose ~71% of D1 actives by D30; a CFPB-cited 2024 survey found 67% who tried a budgeting app called it unhelpful or too much effort. The quit window is **weeks 2–4**.
- **Effort asymmetry decides everything.** Manual apps churn ~3x faster; when sync breaks, ~68% quit rather than reconnect.
- **Plan-forward beats track-backward.** Awareness alone doesn't change behavior; apps that say what to do next retain.
- **Shame kills; permission retains.** Red/over-budget pass-fail framing creates avoidance ("I'm bad at this" → stop opening the app). Percentage/progress framing showed ~43% higher D30 retention.
- **The couple is a retention unit.** Shared visibility is a mutual commitment device — quitting means breaking a shared practice.
- **Light gamification works** (~47% higher 90-day retention for streak mechanics) if rewards attach to real progress.
- **Glanceability:** the dashboard must answer "spent / left / needs attention" in one look.

## 2. Biggest limitations of current apps (ranked severity × frequency)

1. **Bank-sync breakage** — the #1 trust-killer across every aggregator app (disconnects, re-auth loops, 1–3 day lag, unsupported banks). Once numbers are stale, users question the whole system.
2. **Maintenance burden** — tracking becomes homework; backlog guilt; the 2–4 week cliff.
3. **Pricing backlash & subscription fatigue** — YNAB's 2021 hike ($45→$90, now $109/yr), Splitwise's overnight free-tier throttle (~3 entries/day; Trustpilot ~1.8★, ~65% one-star), $100/yr resentment at Monarch/Copilot. Consumers cut paid subscriptions ~32% YoY into 2025.
4. **Platform death & data loss** — Mint (25M users) → Credit Karma migrated no budgets/categories/history; Zeta gave couples ~30 days' notice then permanently deleted shared history; Ivy Wallet abandoned. Users now ask "what happens when this dies?" before committing.
5. **Miscategorization & duplicate toil** — session time goes to fixing data, not learning from it.
6. **Guilt/shame UX** — the red-dashboard death spiral (see retention above).
7. **Feature bloat** — module walls (investments, credit scores, cross-sells) create decision fatigue; users wanted clarity.
8. **Poor couples/multi-user support** — the biggest *structural* gap (next section).
9. **Privacy distrust of bank linking** — Plaid's $58M settlement is cited by name; free apps assumed to monetize data (Mint targeted loan offers off transaction data).
10. **Data lock-in** — weak/no export; leaving an app means losing categorized history.
11. **Data without direction** — charts but no "so what"; contextual guidance made users ~2.4x likelier to change behavior vs data alone.
12. **Support failures & cancellation friction.**

**Churn lifecycle:** honeymoon setup (D0–3) → friction appears: miscategorization, missed entries, first red warnings (W1–2) → the quit window: sync breaks or backlog guilt or "this tells me what I spent, not what to do" (W2–4) → shame spiral, renewal becomes the formal exit (M2–3). Couples variant: hit the merged-vs-separate wall → one partner ends up alone in a spreadsheet → app quietly becomes single-player → dropped.

## 3. The couples / shared segment

- **How couples actually manage money:** ~62% keep at least some money separate (CNBC-reported survey, Jan 2025): ~38% joint-only, ~34% mixed, ~27% fully separate. Gen Z skews hardest toward separate-but-sharing (88% keep something separate). Cohabiting splits: ~⅓ take turns, ~⅓ strict 50/50, ~⅓ income-proportional.
- **The research tension:** UCLA/UCL work on 1,000+ couples found pooled finances correlate with higher relationship satisfaction and lower separation — the mechanism is *psychological togetherness*, not control. Pure debt-ledger UX (Splitwise-style score-keeping) reinforces the "my money vs yours" frame; pure pooling alienates the 62%. **Nobody bridges "fairness" and "ours" in one loop.**
- **Splitwise:** best-in-class settle-up math, but the 2023 free-tier throttle of the *core logging action* triggered a mass exodus (rivals' entire growth strategy is intercepting refugees). Also: every participant must install the app — web-link-first joining is the common wedge of its challengers.
- **Couples-app graveyard:** Zeta (acquired, shut down, data deleted), Honeyfi/Firstly (shut), Tandem (sync delays, $11.99/mo), Honeydue (alive, free, category-best per-account privacy — but visibly unmaintained: frozen features, unanswered support, ads). Demand is proven; no one has survived on a sustainable model.
- **Mainstream shared budgeting:** Monarch's free second seat works but is $99.99/yr and sync-dependent; YNAB Together fails when only one partner is "the finance person" (the typical couple). The everyday two-person loop — *log it, see it, feel even, move on* — is underserved between free-but-dying Honeydue and $100/yr Monarch.

## 4. Market gaps (synthesis across all three sweeps)

1. **The mixed-finances couple** — "some things are ours, some are mine" has no graceful home.
2. **Fairness without score-keeping** — contribution framing (income-proportional or %-of-total) instead of debt framing.
3. **Unthrottled instant manual logging as the core loop** — now a differentiator, post-Splitwise.
4. **Durability / data-ownership promise** — export + local-first + "your ledger outlives us."
5. **A two-person tier between $0 and $100/yr** — one-time or low flat pricing rides subscription fatigue (in categories that offer it, ~77% of consumer spend is still one-time purchases).
6. **India/UPI-era couples tracking** — SMS parsing is structurally dying (Play Store policy + UPI fragmentation); Walnut became a loan-selling surface (Axio); bank apps see only their own accounts. Manual quick-entry PWA fits unusually well.
7. **No-install partner onboarding** — a web link, not an app download.

## 5. Implications for TwoCents

### Bets this research validates (already built)
- **No bank linking** — dodges the #1 complaint source *and* the privacy objection simultaneously. This is the pitch, not a limitation: *no bank permissions, no data leaving your server, works with any bank/UPI app by definition.*
- **3-second unthrottled entry** — attacks the true churn driver (effort), and the exact action Splitwise chose to throttle.
- **Household as the unit + no second-seat cost** — matches Monarch's most-praised differentiator; the couple is the retention unit.
- **PWA** — native no-install partner onboarding; web+mobile (a stated India ask).
- **"Who paid" as contribution %, not debt** — accidentally aligned with the UCLA finding. Keep it this way.
- **Design polish** — a daily-check app must be pleasant to open; this is retention infrastructure, not decoration.

### Highest-leverage additions (ranked by evidence strength × build cost)
1. **CSV export** (trivial build) — directly answers the Mint/Zeta trust wound; part of a "your ledger outlives us" story.
2. **A glanceable pace number** — "₹X so far vs ₹Y typical by the 12th" or safe-to-spend-style daily pace. Answers "so what," the #2 loved feature. **Never red pass/fail framing** — progress/percentage framing only.
3. **Recurring expenses** (auto-log rent/Netflix on schedule) — extends presets; #9 loved feature; kills "surprise charge" pain with zero AI.
4. **Gentle monthly budgets per category** — plan-forward beats track-backward; frame as pace/progress, never shame.
5. **Private-expense flag** — serves the mixed-finances majority: logged and counted in "mine," visible as amount-only (or hidden) to partner. Category-best precedent: Honeydue's per-account privacy controls.
6. **Goals / sinking funds** — the story-with-a-payoff loop (phase 2).
7. **Light streaks/logging feedback** — evidence-backed but keep it subtle (phase 2).

### Things to deliberately NOT do
- No bank/SMS integration — ever the answer to entry friction; it trades our differentiator for the category's #1 complaint.
- No settle-up/debt ledger as the default frame (Splitwise exists for trips; couples research warns against score-keeping).
- No feature-wall dashboard — protect the three-question glance: *spent / left / needs attention*.
- No red pass/fail budget states.
- If/when monetizing: no per-seat pricing for partners, no throttling the logging action, honest one-time or low flat pricing.
